# Prompt for a redesign of the results page

Paste everything below the line into a fresh Claude conversation. Attach a
screenshot of the current page if you can — it reads a lot faster than a
description.

---

I need you to redesign a results page for a data-engineering project. Give me
back the HTML and CSS only; I will put it back into the Python that generates it.

## Who looks at this and why

A hiring manager, over a shared screen, for about twenty seconds, while I talk.
It is not a product dashboard anyone returns to — it is the "so what" slide of a
project walkthrough. If a person cannot tell what the finding is without me
narrating, the page has failed.

The role is a Data Engineering internship. The reader is technical but is reading
for intent and clarity, not for depth.

## What the project is

`Dispatch` is a last-mile delivery pipeline: Kafka → Spark Structured Streaming →
a bronze/silver/gold lake → a star schema in Postgres, orchestrated by Airflow,
with dbt marts and nine blocking data-quality gates. The events are synthetic and
generated by a seeded script, with defects injected on purpose.

## The finding the page exists to deliver

The delivery-promise formula uses one global speed for every store. The stores do
not move at one speed, so the error it leaves behind is more than twice as large
at one store as the other — **and a single pooled average hides that completely.**

    FC002    +10.01 minutes late on average
    FC004     +4.74 minutes late on average
    pooled    +7.79   ← reports one problem where there are two

That contrast is the whole page. Everything else is supporting evidence.

## What is on the page now, in order

1. Header: title, one-line subtitle, a "generated at <time> from the warehouse"
   stamp
2. **The finding** — three big numbers (the two stores and the pooled figure),
   then a horizontal bar chart of signed error per store with a dashed reference
   line at the pooled value, then a caveat paragraph
3. **Breach rate by day** — a two-series line chart over 14 days, with a
   crosshair and tooltip on hover
4. **Per store** — a table: store, trips, mean signed error, MAE, p50, p90,
   breach rate
5. **Pipeline run** — four stat tiles: events landed 31,305 · collapsed in silver
   1,468 · trips 5,000 · quarantined 0
6. **Trip completeness** — a table: complete 4,972 (99.4%), broken 28 (0.6%)
7. **Quality gates** — a table of nine rows: stage, check name, PASS/FAIL badge,
   and a detail sentence

## What is wrong with it

- The bar chart has **two bars** and takes the full page width. Enormous space,
  almost no information.
- It reads like a report printed top to bottom. Nothing pulls the eye to the
  finding first.
- Six sections of roughly equal visual weight, so none of them is the headline.
- On a laptop at 1440px you cannot see the finding and any supporting evidence at
  the same time without scrolling.

## What I want

The finding legible in **under five seconds**, with the strongest supporting
evidence visible on the same screen. Everything else can sit below the fold.

Please think about whether a bar chart is even the right form for comparing three
numbers. I am not attached to it.

## Hard constraints

- **A single self-contained HTML file.** No build step, no framework, no npm.
- **No external requests.** No CDN, no Google Fonts, no remote images. It is
  served from GitHub Pages and must render with the network off.
- **Inline SVG only** for any chart. No charting library.
- **Light and dark mode**, both deliberate. Use `prefers-color-scheme` and also
  support a `data-theme="dark"` attribute on `:root`.
- **Works at phone width** with no horizontal scrolling.
- **Colours must be colourblind-safe** and never the only thing carrying meaning —
  put a direct label next to anything identified by colour.
- The numbers are **injected by Python** via f-string substitution, so keep the
  structure regular and tell me clearly where each value goes. Do not hardcode
  the figures as though they were fixed.
- Watch for `{` and `}` in CSS: the generator uses f-strings, so braces are
  doubled in the source. Just write normal CSS and I will handle the escaping.

## Things not to do

- Do not add a logo, a fake company name, or a navigation bar. It is one page.
- Do not make it look like a SaaS analytics product. It is an engineering result.
- Do not drop the caveat paragraph. The data is synthetic and the page has to say
  so, under the chart it qualifies, not in a footnote.
- Do not remove the quality-gate table. Showing nine checks that all passed is
  part of the point.
- No animation beyond a hover state.
